# Theorem of Kemet — Role-Based Usability Audit

**Audit date:** 26 August 2026  
**Method:** Manual desktop-browser walkthrough of all public routes and non-destructive interactions, followed by a protected-author workflow review from the implemented desk structure and safe preview fallback. No theory letter was submitted, and no case, shelf, letter, or publication state was created, edited, published, or deleted.

> **Scope note:** The live OAuth identity screen requires an Om administrator account. The authentication screen was deliberately not scored or completed. The protected author workflow was reviewed from its implemented UI structure, release rules, existing visual verification, and non-destructive local-preview state.

## Executive Summary

Theorem of Kemet has a distinctive, evidence-aware public experience. A first-time reader can understand that the journal treats mystery as inquiry rather than proof, and the Field Guide supplies the clearest explanation of that editorial promise. The strongest interaction patterns are the archive search, explicit evidence-mode labels, reader display controls, and the two-field theory-letter form.

The primary usability risks are **choice density before content**, **empty-state momentum**, and a few poetic labels whose behavior is not self-evident. Om’s authoring desk has a coherent four-pass structure and strong progressive disclosure. Its main future risk is that archive management will become slower as records and reader letters increase without search, filters, or a direct conversion path from a letter to a draft.

## First-Time Reader Journey

### What worked

The home page clearly presents the archive as Om Nandurkar’s evidence-first curiosity journal. **Enter the archive** is a strong primary action, and the copy consistently distinguishes a question from a conclusion. The Archive filters immediately respond to a natural-language search; searching for “Orion” produced the expected single matching record and updated the visible result count.

The Signal Board is especially careful in its handling of mythic and speculative material. It introduces its evidence modes before readers encounter the card grid and repeatedly explains that alternative explanations should remain visible. The theory-letter form is unusually concise and trustworthy: it explains the two fields, the lack of account creation, and the absence of public comment threads.

### Friction observed

| Priority | Observation | Why it matters | Recommended improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | The first home load begins with a short atmospheric opening sequence before primary content is usable. | A new reader waits before understanding the site or seeing an action. | Keep the sequence, but provide an always-visible skip control or shorten it after first visit. |
| **High** | The navigation presents **Journal**, **Case Index**, and **The Archive** before their distinctions are taught. | New readers may treat these as duplicates and make a low-confidence choice. | Add short labels or a one-line orientation near the first navigation area: *Read essays*, *Browse research files*, and *Search all records*. |
| **Medium** | The Signal Board exposes many filters before a newcomer knows how to choose one. | The variety signals depth but increases first-decision friction. | Add a quiet “Start with evidence mode” or “Browse by question type” prompt above the filter row. |
| **Medium** | “Pull to desk” is evocative but does not predict that it opens an on-page evidence note. | The label asks readers to learn a metaphor before understanding the interaction. | Retain the phrase, but add a visible secondary cue such as *Open evidence note*. |
| **Medium** | The Field Guide is the best explanation of the project’s method but is not central in the first home viewport. | Readers may reach speculative content before learning the editorial standard. | Add a compact Field Guide link beside or under the home hero’s primary archive action. |

## Returning Reader Journey

### What worked

The reader controls are easy to find inside a record. Reading scale, page surface, and quiet motion are grouped in a plain local-preference panel, and the Night ink change applies immediately. The case-file header makes location, estimated reading time, and guiding question visible before the long-form text begins.

The public Case Index and map are honest about missing data. When no cases or coordinates are available, the interfaces explain that nothing is being invented to make the archive look fuller. This is consistent with the product’s central editorial promise.

### Friction observed

| Priority | Observation | Why it matters | Recommended improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | The header search button does not visibly route or focus a search field when used from the Field Guide. | A persistent control appears broken on routes without an `archive-search` input. | Route header search to `/archive` and focus the search field, or hide it where it cannot act. |
| **Medium** | Saving a record provides no apparent active state or confirmation. | Readers cannot tell whether an item was saved locally. | Change the label/icon to a saved state and announce “Saved on this device.” |
| **Medium** | The Reader-mode panel overlaps the article heading at desktop size. | A utility intended to improve reading temporarily obscures the text. | Place it below the header or render it as a narrower anchored panel with a clear close action. |
| **Medium** | Journal, Case Index, and Map show many filters and controls while their live public data returns zero records or pins. | Empty states need a confident next action, not only an explanation. | Add a real sample record, a featured archive link, or a clear “Read a field guide example” action. |
| **Low** | The map canvas loads beneath a zero-pin state. | It creates visual and network weight without advancing the task. | Prefer a static geographic-ledger empty panel until the first eligible pin exists. |

## Om Author Journey

### What worked

The authoring desk’s **Identify → Write → Evidence → Shape & release** sequence is a strong fit for Om’s intended editorial process. The fields are grouped by thought process rather than by database structure. The release bar is continuously available, and it does not claim that scheduled publishing exists. The first-time quick guide reinforces the same four-step model before the form begins.

The Evidence pass cleanly separates claim, record, counterargument, anomaly, and interpretation. The Drive, source-card, and map-pin controls are grouped as optional supporting material. The separate reader preview is safely browser-local and explicitly explains that it never publishes a file.

### Friction observed

| Priority | Observation | Why it matters | Recommended improvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Publish readiness requires title, slug, summary, and body, but does not present an evidence-aware pre-publish reminder. | A finished-looking case can be published without an explicit final check of source or counterargument framing. | Add a non-blocking “Evidence review” checklist in the release bar, including source, counterargument, and uncertainty checks. |
| **Medium** | The full handbook is named as a project Markdown file, not exposed as an in-app reader page. | Om must know the project filesystem to read longer operational guidance. | Add a protected `/om-guide` route or a readable in-app help sheet backed by the handbook’s short form. |
| **Medium** | A growing case list has no visible search or status filter. | Returning to a known draft becomes harder as the archive grows. | Add case search plus draft/published/status filters to the **Return to a file** section. |
| **Medium** | Reader letters cannot become a draft with one deliberate action. | Om must manually re-enter promising prompts into the case desk. | Add a confirmation-based “Start a draft from this letter” action that copies the question only; it should not publish. |
| **Low** | The safe preview route correctly shows an empty fallback when no local draft exists. | Authors may not remember that the preview must be launched from the desk. | Add the preview’s prerequisite to the control label or helper text: *Preview this current desk draft*. |

## Recommended Improvement Order

| Order | Improvement | Reason to prioritize now |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Repair or contextualize the global header search. | It is a visible interaction that currently lacks a reliable outcome on several routes. |
| 2 | Add evidence-aware pre-publish guidance. | It reinforces the product’s core promise at the irreversible decision point. |
| 3 | Improve empty-state routes with one strong, real next action. | The journal currently has no published records in the reviewed data state, so empty states shape early perception. |
| 4 | Add author search/filtering and draft-from-letter conversion. | These protect Om’s workflow as the archive and inbox grow. |
| 5 | Make the Field Guide more prominent for first-time readers. | It clarifies the site’s cultural and evidentiary frame before speculation-led discovery. |

## Audit Protocol for Future Updates

Update this file with the date, tested routes, observed results, and any unresolved friction.
